## Waypoint – Youth Mountain Biking Training Hub

Waypoint is a user-centered web application designed to help competitive youth mountain bikers organize their training, set and track goals, monitor their progress, and stay connected with their team.

The concept for Waypoint was developed through a team-based human-centered design process. Our team conducted interviews with youth mountain bikers to better understand their training experiences, goals, frustrations, and interactions with their teams. We translated our research into user personas, journey maps, prototypes, and iterative designs, using feedback from real users to guide our design decisions.

After completing the research and design process with my team, I independently developed the website from scratch using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, translating our design concepts into a functional web application. While the current implementation does not fully match the visual fidelity of our final prototypes, it incorporates the core features and interactions that emerged from our research.

## Design Process

Our team followed a user-centered design process that included:

* Conducting interviews with youth mountain bikers
* Identifying recurring needs, behaviors, and pain points from our research
* Developing **user personas** based on our target users
* Creating **journey maps** to understand the youth mountain biking experience
* Brainstorming and evaluating potential solutions
* Creating and iterating on prototypes based on our findings
* Refining the final product concept around the needs identified through user research

## Development

Following the collaborative research and design process, I independently built the functional implementation of Waypoint.

I used:

* HTML to structure the application and its content
* CSS to build the layout, responsive interface, navigation, cards, and visual system
* JavaScript to implement the application's interactions and dynamic behavior
* OpenWeather API to incorporate real-time weather information for practice and race locations

I implemented the website's navigation, activity tracking, interactive training calendar, goal and waypoint system, modal workflows, team tabs, progress history, and weather functionality.

## Key Features

**Training Plan**

Athletes can view activities for different training days and mark individual activities as completed.

**Waypoints & Goal Setting**

Athletes can break larger season goals into smaller waypoints, view completed, current, and upcoming goals, mark waypoints as complete, and select their next milestone.

**Progress & History**

A history dashboard provides statistics for practices, races, completed activities, and achieved waypoints. Athletes can also use an interactive calendar to look back at previous activities.

**Team Hub**

Athletes can access coach updates, teammate shoutouts, practice schedules, race schedules, and other team information in one place.

**Trail Weather**

Athletes can search a practice or race location to view current weather conditions and receive basic guidance about whether conditions are suitable for riding.

## What I Learned

This project gave me experience with both sides of product development: understanding what users need and then independently building a technical solution around those needs.

Through the collaborative research and design process, I gained experience conducting user interviews, synthesizing research, developing personas and journey maps, prototyping, and iterating on designs. I learned how research findings can be translated into specific product requirements rather than designing features based solely on assumptions.

Independently developing the website then challenged me to turn those designs into a functional product. Building the application from scratch strengthened my skills in HTML, CSS, JavaScript, responsive design, DOM manipulation, API integration, and interactive front-end development while showing me how technical implementation can affect the original design vision.

## Future Improvements

The current website primarily serves as a functional implementation of the features developed through our design process. Future improvements would include bringing the interface closer to our final high-fidelity prototypes, adding persistent user accounts and data storage, improving accessibility and responsiveness, and conducting additional usability testing with youth mountain bikers to further refine the experience.
